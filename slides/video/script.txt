We picked a benchmark the frontier hasn't solved: Zapier's AutomationBench, real business workflows graded on the final app state. Frontier models started below ten percent, and the best today is about fifty. Our bar: Kimi K3, at forty-seven percent.
Instead of betting on one trick, we combined three, all on Fireworks: a Holo-style harness on DeepSeek; Holo4 distilled into Qwen and NVIDIA Nemotron with LoRA; and a router that sends each task to the system that solved similar ones. Together: fifty-eight percent versus Kimi's forty-seven, at less than half the cost.
Lever one, the harness: an operating procedure, and a verify-before-finishing step. Our first version actually hurt. Version two, written only from calibration failures, scores fifty-eight percent.
Lever two, distillation. We LoRA fine-tuned on two hundred fifty-nine successful Holo4 runs, in five minutes and about eleven dollars per model. Qwen jumped from seven to thirty-two percent, and Nemotron from twelve to twenty-eight.
Lever three, the router. Embed the task, find the seven most similar calibration tasks, and pick the system that did best on them. It never sees the test set. Perfect routing reaches seventy percent, so there's real headroom.
On sixty held-out tasks, the router scores fifty-eight point three percent versus Kimi K3's forty-six point seven, at six cents per task instead of fourteen: two point two times cheaper. With sixty tasks, it's promising, not yet proven.
Lessons: combine levers, tune harnesses on real failures, and distillation is cheap but limited by coverage. Next: more demonstrations and smarter routing, toward seventy percent. All open models, all on Fireworks. Thank you!
